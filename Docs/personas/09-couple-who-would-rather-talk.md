# 09 — Fiona and Dev, who would rather talk to each other

**Who.** 36 and 38, married nine years, four days in Paris and the first trip without the
children. They booked a tour because they wanted a shape to the afternoon, not because they wanted
to be told things. **What they are actually here to do is finish about six conversations they have
been starting for two years.** The tour is competing with that, and it will lose whenever the
competition is direct.

**The request.** Start Place Dauphine, open walk, three hours, interest: whatever is worth it.
Sunday, 15:00, dry and cool.

**What they need and never type in.** Long stretches with nothing being said. Somewhere to sit
that is quiet enough to hear each other. And short pieces — a good five minutes beats an excellent
nine.

## The three hours

1. 15:00–15:12 — Twelve minutes on Place Dauphine, reading a menu and deciding where to eat later.
   The tour has not begun. **The app thinks it started at 15:00 and it is already four minutes and
   two stops out of step by the time they walk.**
2. 15:12–15:20 — Onto the Pont Neuf, listening. Eight minutes and the audio is good. Neither of
   them says anything.
3. 15:20–15:38 — Eighteen minutes standing on the bridge. The narration ran five. **For the other
   thirteen they talk about the school Dev's mother mentioned on the phone**, which is the single
   most useful thing that happens all week.
4. 15:38–15:44 — Walking. Dev pauses the tour without mentioning it, mid-sentence, because a voice
   in his ear is now in the way. **The pause is not an interruption of the product. It is the
   product being used correctly**, and from here the app's finish time is fiction.
5. 15:44–16:00 — Cour Carrée. Sixteen minutes: six of audio, then ten walking slowly round the
   courtyard talking about it. Camille spends 33 here and Théo 8. **They spend 16 and only 6 of it
   is the tour.**
6. 16:00–16:08 — Out through the Passage Richelieu and across to the Palais-Royal. Audio still
   paused; nobody has thought about it.
7. 16:08–16:40 — Thirty-two minutes in the Palais-Royal garden on two green chairs. Four minutes
   of audio at the start, then a conversation about whether to move house. **This is the best stop
   of the afternoon and almost none of it is the tour.** What made it the best stop is seating,
   enclosure and quiet — three properties no POI in the corpus carries.
8. 16:40–16:47 — Walk to the Galerie Vivienne.
9. 16:47–17:02 — Fifteen minutes. The audio goes back on because they have run out of things to
   say for the moment, and because the gallery is loud enough that talking there is effort.
   **They restart the tour when the conversation pauses, not when the plan expects them to.**
10. 17:02–17:06 — Four minutes round to the Rue de Richelieu.
11. 17:06–17:14 — Eight minutes at the old Bibliothèque Nationale. The piece is nine minutes long.
    They walk off at eight. **Whatever was in the last minute, they will never hear** — and the
    last minute is where a well-written piece puts its point.
12. 17:14–17:32 — Eighteen minutes walking down to the Tuileries, in no hurry, talking the whole
    way. A brisk router would call this leg eleven minutes and be wrong by seven.
13. 17:32–17:52 — Twenty minutes on chairs by the round pond. No audio at all. They never turn it
    back on.
14. 17:52–18:00 — Eight minutes out to the Rue de Rivoli and dinner.

## The arithmetic

**Walking: 59 minutes. At places: 121 minutes.** Six of their seven walking legs are silent by
choice. Audio published across the stops they reached: about 42 minutes. Audio they actually
listened to: about 37. **Audio they would have called ideal: about 25.**

## What this persona breaks

- **Silence has to be schedulable, not merely tolerated.** Camille's silence is a by-product of
  standing somewhere for longer than the narration lasts, and the model can accommodate that by
  accident. Fiona and Dev want the tour to be *deliberately quiet* on a named leg and to come back
  afterwards. **There is no way to express "not here".**
- **The pause button is the primary control and it invalidates the plan.** They use it five times
  and never once resume where the schedule assumed. Marcus (`04-layover-sprinter.md`) would miss
  his train on this behaviour; these two do not care, but the plan has to survive being suspended
  repeatedly by people who will not tell it why.
- **A stop can be abandoned mid-narration** (step 11). Nadia abandons a whole tour; these two
  abandon individual pieces halfway through, routinely. **Content placed at the end of a long piece
  is content nobody in this party receives**, which is an argument about writing that the planner
  currently has no reason to care about.
- **A shorter piece is a better piece here, on purpose.** Théo wants twelve minutes at Concorde
  where Camille wants four. These two want four where the corpus has nine. A stop's narration must
  be able to be short *by intent*, distinguishable from short *because the beats ran out*.
- **A place's worth includes whether it is good to talk in.** The Palais-Royal garden wins the
  afternoon on chairs, enclosure and quiet. The Galerie Vivienne earns its audio by being too loud
  to converse in. **The corpus records only a yes or no for whether two people can sit and talk
  there**, and for this party that fact outranks every cultural property.
- **One request, one duration, one interest — and two people who disagree.** Dev would have kept
  walking at step 7; Fiona wanted to sit. Every persona before this one is a single decision-maker
  or a parent with authority. A couple negotiates, and the negotiation costs minutes the plan never
  allocated.
