## THE TWO MISSING MINUTES — what I'd actually reach for

Ranked by how much each earns its seconds against your three lenses. Every one is a *turn*, not a
statistic.

1. **Radio Row was destroyed to rescue a bankrupt railroad.** New Jersey would only agree to the
   World Trade Center on the West Side if the Port Authority took over the failing Hudson & Manhattan
   line. It did, and PATH runs under your feet today. Four hundred merchants were condemned so a
   commuter railway could be saved. (F15, F2, F17) — **HH**
2. **"Here Lies Mr. Small Businessman."** 14 July 1962, a black-draped coffin carried down Cortlandt
   Street. They lost in the New York Court of Appeals and the Supreme Court dismissed the appeal for
   want of a substantial federal question. The compensation was $3,000 a business; by February 1966
   only 32 had taken it. (F6, F7, F8, F10) — **HH**
3. **The hole under you is the neighbourhood next door.** 1.2 million cubic yards came out of this
   bathtub and went into the Hudson behind a cofferdam. That is Battery Park City. (F75) — **HH/HA**
4. **You are standing on a roof.** Heavy steel framing that is simultaneously the plaza, the museum's
   ceiling and the chiller plant's — carrying six feet of manufactured soil and four hundred one-ton
   oaks, over the PATH line. (F56, F57, F58, F62) — **MCD/HH**
5. **The trees drink the rain that falls on you.** The whole plaza is engineered as one cistern; the
   same recycled water runs the waterfalls. The paving is dry-laid in sand so it can be lifted and
   put back. (F63, F64, F65) — **MCD**
6. **The bronze is temperature-controlled so it never burns or freezes a hand.** Glycol through
   14,000 feet of half-inch copper, holding the panels between 40 and 70 °F — because the memorial
   only works if you can touch it. Ronaldo Vega called it "almost like a baptismal setting."
   (F20, F21, F22) — **MCD**
7. **The site had twin towers before.** The Hudson Terminal, 1908, Clinton & Russell — twin 22-storey
   towers over the railway station, briefly the largest office buildings in the world, demolished for
   the ones you are standing between. (F16) — **HA/MCD**
8. **The towers were never actually twins.** Only the North Tower carried the 360-ft mast; the South
   was designed for an identical one that was never built. And the strike zones differed — the North
   Tower's severed every escape route above it; in the South, some people above the impact got out.
   That is why these two identical pools are not the same place. (F32, §1c item 4) — **HH**
9. **The gothic arches were a structural necessity.** Perimeter columns at 3 ft 4 in on centre had to
   merge into fewer, wider ones to open a lobby door — ASCE calls the merge the "tree junctions." The
   most recognisable thing about the towers was the price of a doorway. (F36) — **HA/MCD**
10. **1,776 feet was a decision, not a measurement.** A CTBUH committee in Chicago ruled the mast a
    spire rather than an antenna; David Childs argued it in person. And "104 storeys" is a numbering
    convention — CTBUH counts 94 floors. (F43, F44) — **MCD**

# PART 2 — THE FACTS

## 2a. WHAT STOOD HERE BEFORE — RADIO ROW

> **Two corrections to the brief before anything else.**
> **(i) The case is not "Courtland Street Corp. v. Port of New York Authority."** No such case exists.
> The merchants' case is ***Courtesy Sandwich Shop, Inc. v. Port of New York Authority*** — named for a
> lunch counter, not a radio store.
> **(ii) The Supreme Court did not "decline to hear" it.** This was a mandatory appeal: the Court
> *granted a motion to dismiss and dismissed the appeal for want of a substantial federal question* —
> a disposition with precedential weight, unlike a cert denial.

**F1.** The World Trade Center superblock "ran south from Vesey Street to Liberty Street and west from
Church Street to West Street" — 16 acres, replacing roughly **13 square blocks**.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "World Peace Through World Trade" (WTC History digital exhibition) — https://www.911memorial.org/learn/resources/digital-exhibitions/world-trade-center-history/world-peace-through-world-trade
LENS: HH

**F2.** The district's core sat at **Cortlandt and Greenwich Streets**, "roughly bounded by Dey Street
to the north, Liberty to the south, between West and Church Streets," with **"over 400 merchants"** at
peak. Dey Street was also known as **Telegram Square**.
[ARCHIVE] NYC Dept. of Records & Information Services, Michael Lorenzini, "Radio Row and the Fight for Lower Manhattan," 2024-01-05 — https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/1/5/radio-row-and-the-fight-for-lower-manhattan
LENS: HH

**F3. — DO NOT USE "325 BUSINESSES".** No source anywhere supports 325. The published figures are
**"more than 300 businesses and 30,000 employees"** (the institution), "over 300 street-level stores"
(press), and "over 400 merchants" (Municipal Archives). The 300/400 gap is real and meaningful: 300
counts *street-level* shops; 400+ counts all enterprises, and one press account says there were "over
three times as many enterprises in the floors above" the 300 street-level stores.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "World Peace Through World Trade" — URL at F1
LENS: HH

**F4. — THE 30,000 FIGURE IS AN ADVOCACY CLAIM, NOT A COUNT.** It originates with **Oscar Nadel**, the
merchants' leader, who asserted the threatened area held "1,600 commercial businesses doing an annual
business volume of $300,000,000 and employing about 30,000 persons" — a figure for *all* businesses in
the 13 blocks, not the electronics trade. Institutions have repeated it since without re-deriving it.
**Phrase it as "the merchants claimed," never as "30,000 people worked there."**
[INST] New York Preservation Archive Project, "Radio Row" — https://www.nypap.org/preservation-history/radio-row/
LENS: HH

**F5. — THE ASSOCIATION'S NAME IS WRONG IN THE BRIEF.** There was no "Downtown West Side Merchants'
Association." Two real names appear in the record: the **Downtown West Businessmen's Association**,
formed 1959 and run out of Oscar Nadel's shop at **63 Cortlandt Street**; and the **Downtown West Small
Business Survival Committee**.
[PRESS] Syd Steinhardt, "The Death of New York's Radio Row," *Downtown Express*, 2002-03-05 — https://www.qcwa.org/radio-row.htm
[INST] NYPAP, "Radio Row" — https://www.nypap.org/preservation-history/radio-row/
LENS: HH

**F6. — THE COFFIN.** **14 July 1962**: "Nadel and his Survival Committee parade a coffin to protest the
World Trade Center." Merchants marched along Cortlandt, Dey and Liberty Streets with a black-draped
coffin bearing the sign **"Here Lies Mr. Small Businessman."**
[INST] NYPAP, "Radio Row" — https://www.nypap.org/preservation-history/radio-row/
[PRESS] Oliver E. Allen, "Before the World Trade Center, the Long Lost World of Radio Row," *Tribeca Trib*, orig. Oct 2002 — https://tribecatrib.com/content/world-trade-center-long-lost-world-radio-row
LENS: HH

**F7.** The New York Court of Appeals upheld the condemnation in ***Courtesy Sandwich Shop, Inc. v. Port
of New York Authority*, 12 N.Y.2d 379 (1963)**, decided **4 April 1963**. Judge Burke wrote for the
majority; **Judge Van Voorhis dissented**. The holding: "any use of the property sought to be condemned
that is functionally related to the centralizing of all port business is unobjectionable even though
private persons are to be the immediate lessees."
[COURT] *Courtesy Sandwich Shop, Inc. v. Port of New York Authority*, 12 N.Y.2d 379, 240 N.Y.S.2d 1, 190 N.E.2d 402 — https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/courtesy-sandwich-shop-inc-884498461
LENS: HH

**F8. — HOW IT ENDED.** **12 November 1963**, docket No. 399: *"The motion to dismiss is granted and the
appeal is dismissed for want of a substantial federal question."* Rehearing denied 16 December 1963.
The merchants' counsel included **Morris L. Ernst**, the ACLU co-founder.
[COURT] *Courtesy Sandwich Shop, Inc. v. Port of New York Authority*, 375 U.S. 78 (1963), official *United States Reports*, U.S. GPO — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-375/pdf/USREPORTS-375-78.pdf
Corroborating: [INST] Cornell LII — https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/375/78
LENS: HH

**F9.** The bistate mechanism the courts deferred to was **Chapter 209 of the New York Laws of 1962**,
enacted in parallel with New Jersey legislation, authorising the Port Authority to build the WTC and
to condemn for it.
[COURT] *Courtesy Sandwich Shop*, 12 N.Y.2d 379 — URL at F7
LENS: HH

**F10. — THE $3,000.** The Port Authority offered **each displaced business $3,000** to relocate. "Some
moved, but most went out of business." By **28 February 1966 only 32 business owners had accepted**.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "A Look Back at Manhattan's Radio Row" — https://www.911memorial.org/connect/blog/look-back-manhattans-radio-row
[PRESS] Steinhardt, *Downtown Express*, 2002 — https://www.qcwa.org/radio-row.htm
LENS: HH

**F11.** First condemnations filed **14 September 1965**. Demolition began **21 March 1966**, when the
**Ajax Wrecking and Lumber Company** destroyed six buildings on **Dey Street**. WTC construction began
**5 August 1966**. *(Steinhardt dates the first demolitions 26 March 1966 — a five-day discrepancy I
could not adjudicate against a permit record.)*
[INST] NYPAP, "Radio Row" — https://www.nypap.org/preservation-history/radio-row/
[INST] 9/11 Memorial, "World Trade Center Facts and Figures" — https://www.911memorial.org/learn/resources/digital-exhibitions/world-trade-center-history/world-trade-center-facts-and-figures
LENS: HH

**F12. — NAMED PEOPLE, ALL ON CORTLANDT STREET.** **Oscar Nadel**, Oscar's Radio Shop, 63 Cortlandt (a
storefront that had been DaVega City Radio in the 1930s); **Irving Jaffe**, who opened East Radio at 74
Cortlandt in 1933; **Herb Simonoff**, manager of Leonard Radio at 65 Cortlandt. In June 1965 Nadel
testified that **10 percent of all radio, television, stereo and hi-fi sets sold by American
manufacturers were sold to retailers in this district.**
[PRESS] Steinhardt, *Downtown Express*, 2002 — https://www.qcwa.org/radio-row.htm
[ARCHIVE] NYC Municipal Archives, Lorenzini, 2024-01-05 — https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/1/5/radio-row-and-the-fight-for-lower-manhattan
LENS: HH

**F13. — THE BEST HUMAN DETAIL IN THE WHOLE FILE.** The Municipal Archives holds the **Jean M. Brown
collection**: photographs a resident of **189 Greenwich Street** took of the demolition before her own
eviction in **November 1966, seven months pregnant**. She donated them in 2005. The Archives also holds
1939–40 Tax Photographs of individual Radio Row storefronts (DaVega City Radio at 63 Cortlandt,
North-Star Radio at 78-80, Bell Radio at 60, Vim Radio at 70, Arrow Radio at 82, Leotone Radio at 65-67
Dey) and WPA Federal Writers' Project photographs.
[ARCHIVE] NYC Dept. of Records & Information Services, Lorenzini, 2024-01-05 — https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/1/5/radio-row-and-the-fight-for-lower-manhattan
LENS: HH

**F14. — THE CRITICS WERE NOT ONLY THE MERCHANTS.** *Harper's Magazine*, May 1966, ran Wolf Von
Eckardt's "New York's Trade Center: World's Tallest Fiasco," calling the plan an "instrument of
urbicide … not only the tallest, but unquestionably one of the ugliest buildings in the world," which
would displace "thirteen historic, living and breathing square blocks of the city."
[ARCHIVE] NYC Municipal Archives, Lorenzini, quoting *Harper's Magazine*, May 1966 — https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/1/5/radio-row-and-the-fight-for-lower-manhattan
*(The Harper's original is paywalled — HTTP 402 — so this is quoted at one remove from a city archive.)*
LENS: MCD / HH

**F15. — WHY IT WAS HERE AT ALL (the connective tissue).** The **Downtown-Lower Manhattan Association**
was founded **1958** with **David Rockefeller** as first chairman; its "First Report," **14 October
1958**, proposed a world trade complex. By 1960 the plan was for the **East River**, between Fulton St.
and Old Slip. New Jersey's Governor **Robert Meyner** wanted it on the **West Side** for New Jersey
commuters; **Nelson A. Rockefeller**, David's brother and Governor of New York, struck the deal — the
Port Authority would take over the bankrupt **Hudson & Manhattan Railroad** and build PATH. **27 March
1962**: the two governors signed. **Radio Row was destroyed because a railroad had to be rescued.**
[ARCHIVE] Rockefeller Archive Center, Michele Beckerman & Andrea Cadornigara, "Photo Essay: Building the World Trade Center in Lower Manhattan," 2024-09-11 — https://resource.rockarch.org/story/photo-essay-building-the-world-trade-center-in-lower-manhattan/
LENS: HH

**F16. — THE SITE HAD TWIN TOWERS BEFORE.** The **Hudson Terminal** buildings — twin **22-storey**
office towers over a three-level Hudson & Manhattan station at **30-50 Church Street at Fulton** —
were completed **1908** to designs by **Clinton & Russell**, and were for a time the largest office
buildings in the world. Thirty-nine elevators; bridges linking them at the third and seventeenth
floors; station entrances off Cortlandt, Fulton and two off Dey.
[ARCHIVE] Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division, "Hudson Terminal Bldgs., 30-50 Church St," Irving Underhill, c1908, LOT 3788 box 10 — https://www.loc.gov/item/00652958/ *(catalogue accessible via the LOC JSON API; the HTML page 403s to automated fetchers)*
[INST] Darius Sollohub (NJIT), "The Twins before the Twin Towers: the Hudson Terminals and their Bifurcated Form," *ACSA Annual Meeting Proceedings* 93, 342–346 — https://www.acsa-arch.org/proceedings/Annual%20Meeting%20Proceedings/ACSA.AM.93/ACSA.AM.93.42.pdf
LENS: HA / MCD

**F17.** The **H&M Railroad** opened **25 February 1908**, when **President Theodore Roosevelt pressed a
button from the White House** to energise the system; downtown service to the Hudson Terminal followed
**19 July 1909**. The Port Authority acquired it in **1962** and created PATH.
[INST] Port Authority of NY & NJ, Roz Hamlett, "Before PATH Became PATH: The Wonder Years," *Portfolio*, 2016-06-03 — https://portfolio.panynj.gov/2016/06/03/before-path-became-path-the-wonder-years/
LENS: HA / HH

**F18. — WHAT WAS HERE BEFORE RADIO ROW.** The **Washington Market** replaced the 1771 Bear Market on
the west side of Greenwich Street between Fulton and Vesey in **1812**, expanded 1843/1847/1853, and by
the 1880s was New York's major wholesale and retail produce outlet. The LPC's own footnote: "Demolition
of the market and surrounding buildings took place in the late 1960s; the site of the Washington Market
is now partially occupied by the World Trade Center."
[INST] NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission, *Tribeca West Historic District Designation Report*, May 1991 (LP-1713), footnote 21 — https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1713.pdf
LENS: HA / HH

**F19. — A LINE YOU CAN USE AS ITSELF.** The Port Authority does not publicly narrate the displacement
it caused. Its own public WTC history page carries no Radio Row content, and its 1962–66 annual reports
are not online. Every figure about the merchants comes from the merchants, the press, the city's
archives, or the 9/11 Memorial — never from the authority that condemned them.
[INST] Port Authority of NY & NJ, WTC history page — https://wtcprod.panynj.gov/en/local/learn-about-wtc/history.html (verified: renders as navigation only; no Radio Row content)
LENS: HH

## 2b. THE SLURRY WALL — "THE BATHTUB"

**F71. — WHY IT EXISTS.** The Port Authority had to build the world's two tallest buildings on **16
acres of landfill that had once been submerged in the Hudson River**. The solution was to build "an
underground perimeter wall that would **stop the entry of river water into the site and permit
excavation down to bedrock**."
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "Slurry Wall: Behind the Engineering Feat That Made the WTC Possible" — https://www.911memorial.org/connect/blog/slurry-wall-behind-engineering-feat-made-wtc-possible
LENS: HA / HH

**F72. — THE METHOD IS ITALIAN AND IT IS YOUNGER THAN YOU THINK.** "The slurry wall method was
**patented in Italy in the late 1940s by the ICOS Company**." It was first used in **Milan's subway
system in the 1950s**, reached the United States on small projects in the 1960s, and was employed at
the World Trade Center in **1967** — at a scale never before attempted in America.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial, "Slurry Wall" blog — URL at F71
LENS: HA / MCD

**F73. — THE DIMENSIONS, FROM THE INSTITUTION.** The wall runs **70 feet down to bedrock**; individual
panels were "**3 feet wide, 22 feet long, and 70 feet deep**"; the perimeter wall is **3,500 feet**
long; and **approximately 158 panels enclosed 11 of the 16 acres**.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial, "Slurry Wall" blog — URL at F71
*Spread: ASCE gives the trench as "3 ft thick, 22 ft wide, and 70 ft deep" — the same panel, with
"wide" and "long" swapped. The commonly cited "152 panels" is NOT the institution's figure; it says
~158. (152 is the number of bronze parapet sections — the two numbers get confused.)*
LENS: HA

**F74. — HOW IT IS HELD BACK.** "**Six-inch-diameter casings are drilled diagonally through openings
cast in the wall**" and anchored into bedrock with steel tendons that are "tensioned, tested for
strength, and locked." ASCE counts approximately **1,500 total high-strength tiebacks**, anchored **30
to 35 ft** into bedrock.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial, "Slurry Wall" blog — URL at F71
[INST] Leslie Nemo, "The Twin Towers: One-of-a-kind made twice," *Civil Engineering* (ASCE), Sept/Oct 2026 — https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2026/09/the-twin-towers-one-of-a-kind-made-twice
LENS: HA

**F75. — THE BEST HIDDEN-HISTORY FACT ON THIS ENTIRE SITE.** The excavation — "the bathtub" — covered
**16 acres** and required removing **more than 1.2 million cubic yards of soil and rock**. That spoil
was "dumped behind a cellular steel cofferdam" in the Hudson, creating **about 23.5 acres** of new
land: **Battery Park City**. The hole under your feet is the neighbourhood next door.
[INST] ASCE, *Civil Engineering*, Sept/Oct 2026 — URL at F74
LENS: HH / HA

**F76. — IT HELD.** After the towers collapsed and the debris was cleared in **spring 2002**, the
**70-foot-deep** wall stood exposed. Despite considerable damage it **did not fail**. **Peter
Rinaldi**, the Port Authority engineer, "played a key role in stabilizing and preserving the slurry
wall" during the recovery.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "Public Program at the 9/11 Memorial Museum to Highlight Unique History of the Slurry Wall" — https://www.911memorial.org/connect/blog/public-program-911-memorial-museum-highlight-unique-history-slurry-wall
LENS: HH

**F77. — THE LINE THAT TURNED IT INTO ARCHITECTURE.** Daniel Libeskind, whose master plan made
preserving the exposed wall a founding move: "**The foundations withstood the unimaginable trauma of
the destruction and stand as eloquent as the Constitution itself.**"
[INST] 9/11 Memorial, "Public Program … Slurry Wall" — URL at F76
LENS: MCD / HA

**F78. — IT IS DIRECTLY BENEATH YOU, AND YOU CAN SAY SO.** The institution's own words: "The largest
space in the Museum is **Foundation Hall, situated beside the North Tower footprint**. The backdrop for
Foundation Hall is **a monumental portion of the slurry wall**, a concrete retaining wall built to hold
back the waters of the nearby Hudson River when the World Trade Center site was first excavated in the

## 2g. THE POOLS AND THE FOOTPRINT QUESTION — ANSWERED HONESTLY

**F49.** The institution: two pools, **each nearly an acre**, water **descending 30 feet** into a square
basin then dropping **another 20 feet** into a centre void; they "contain the **largest manmade

**F51. — THE CONTESTED POINT, STATED HONESTLY.** No source — institutional or designer — documents a

## 2h. THE GLADE — a plaza stop the official audio guide makes and your sheet does not

**F52.** The **9/11 Memorial Glade** was dedicated **30 May 2019**, timed to the **17th anniversary of
the formal end of rescue and recovery operations at Ground Zero**. It honours "all who are sick or who
have died from exposure to toxins in the aftermath of the 2001 attacks at all three attack sites."
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum press release, "9/11 Memorial Glade Opens to the Public," 2019-05-30 — https://www.911memorial.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/may_30_release_glade_dedication_final.pdf
LENS: HH

**F53. — WHERE IT IS IS THE POINT.** The Glade is "located just **west of the Survivor Tree**, roughly
**where the primary ramp that was used during the rescue and recovery effort once stood**." Six stone
monoliths, **13 to 18 tons** each, granite quarried and shaped in **Barre, Vermont**, **inlaid with
World Trade Center steel melted down and formed to fit the fissures**. Project cost approximately
**$5 million**. Designed by **Arad and Walker**, the original designers — the only alteration to their
own memorial.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial press release, 2019-05-30 — URL at F52
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "9/11 Memorial Glade" — https://www.911memorial.org/visit/memorial/glade
LENS: MCD / HH

**F54. — THE DESIGNER'S OWN WORDS, WHICH ARE USABLE VERBATIM.** Michael Arad: "I am incredibly grateful
for the opportunity that was given to us **to rectify the design of the 9/11 Memorial** to include a
long overdue tribute … we didn't recognize the cost of that heroism till years after the fact. … Its
**roughhewn monoliths** convey the strength and determination shown by this remarkable group, and the
World Trade Center steel that is embedded in these monoliths alludes to the sinewy strength and
determination they displayed in the face of adversity. **As Hemingway wrote, we are stronger in our
broken places.**"
[INST] 9/11 Memorial press release, 2019-05-30 — URL at F52
LENS: MCD

**F55.** The six monoliths were installed on **6 April 2019** using a **600-ton crane** plus an assist
crane, each lifted **over the 30-to-35-foot-tall swamp white oaks** already on the plaza.
[INST] 9/11 Memorial & Museum, "Monoliths Installed on 9/11 Memorial Glade" — https://www.911memorial.org/connect/blog/monoliths-installed-911-memorial-glade

# PART 4 — COULD NOT VERIFY

**Radio Row began in 1921 with Harry Schneck's City Radio.** This is family oral history, not a
document — the Downtown Alliance attributes it to Schneck's son. What *is* documented is the first
appearance of the name in print: the *New York Times*, **24 July 1927** ("RADIO JUBILEE IS PLANNED FOR
AUTUMN; Dealers Along 'Radio Row'…") and **6 September 1927** ("'RADIO ROW' BEGINS ITS JUBILEE TODAY;
Cortlandt Street, Gay With Flags and Bunting…"). Headlines and dates confirmed via Barry Popik's
etymology file; **the NYT originals were not fetchable**. Treat 1921 as tradition, 1927 as the
documented first citation, and say which is which.

**The street list "Cortlandt, Dey, Greenwich, Washington, Fulton, Barclay."** Sources agree on
Cortlandt, Dey, Greenwich and Barclay. **Washington and Fulton belong to the Washington Market's
footprint** (F18), not the radio trade's core. Do not merge the two lists.

**Whether the Appellate Division ruled for or against the Port Authority.** A search summary asserted
the First Department ruled against the Port Authority on public purpose (17 A.D.2d 590); the opinion
could not be opened (Justia 403, CourtListener JS-only, Casetext blocked). The line sometimes quoted as
the holding — that injecting the Port Authority into a real-estate project "was outside of the original
compact" — reads like **Van Voorhis's dissent**. Do not use it as the court's view.

**HABS documentation of Radio Row, the Hudson Terminal or the Washington Market.** None exists in the
Library of Congress HABS collection. What LOC does hold is **commercial photography** — the Irving
Underhill Hudson Terminal print (F16) and a Detroit Publishing Co. series
(https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2016810285/). If you want images, that is the collection, not HABS.

**Port Authority annual reports 1962–1966.** Not online in any form that could be opened. See F19 —
the absence is itself usable.

**The typeface of the names.** No source identifies it. Press and forum chatter guess; none cites a
designer or foundry. (The Gotham typeface is documented for the **2004 cornerstone inscription**, a
different object.) **Do not name a typeface.** What *is* documented is the letterform *engineering*:
stencil-cut counters filled with machined wafers, 14 extrusion profiles (F25).

**Who designed the parapet temperature system.** Two credible sources disagree. The Copper Development
Association says **JB&B with KC Fabrications** (naming Christopher Powers of KC as co-designer, F20);
**Handel Architects' own page says the system was "designed by New Jersey-based Service Metal
Fabricating."** ENR names **JB&B** as MEP engineer. Safest phrasing: "the memorial's engineers," or
name **JB&B**, which all three support.

**The $3.5M budgeted / $4.3M final cost of the parapet system.** Traceable only to the *New York Post*
via Untapped New York; no institutional or trade source carries either number. `[PRESS]` at one remove.
**Do not use a dollar figure for the parapets.**

**The pools at exactly 176 ft square.** The only *measured* figure found is the weir fabricator's
"just over 173 feet" per side (F50). WSP says 200 ft × 200 ft; ENR says 31,264 sq ft (≈177 ft square);
CDA says the pools are "45 feet in depth" against the institution's 30 ft + 20 ft. **The pool
dimensions are genuinely unsettled across four credible sources. Use "about an acre each" and "thirty
feet down, then twenty more."**

**1 WTC "opened for tenants November 3, 2014."** No institutional page states this. SOM and CTBUH say
only "completed 2014." Treat the date as unsourced.

**1 WTC's base being 200 ft × 200 ft, "the same as the original towers."** Only forbidden-source types
assert it, and it is likely false as stated: NIST puts the original footprint at ~210 ft exterior
(F33), so a 200-ft base would be *smaller*, not identical. SOM gives no base dimension.

**The 1WTC spire at 408 ft.** SOM's own site says **441 ft**. The 1,792 ft tip minus 1,776 ft
architectural top is only 16 ft, so neither figure is simply "top minus roof." Unresolved — prefer
SOM's 441 ft or omit.

**The Oculus at ~$4bn against a ~$2bn budget.** Press only (no Port Authority page carries either
figure). If used, mark `[PRESS]` and give a range: ~$2bn announced in 2004, $3.7–4.0bn final.
