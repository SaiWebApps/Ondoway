# 9/11 Memorial — the design argument
Sourced facts for a 30-second beat, lens: Modern & Contemporary Design.
Researched 2026-09-23. Web only.

**Provenance key used throughout:**
- `[INST]` = the institution's own claim (911memorial.org, or the LMDC jury statement it hosts)
- `[DESIGNER]` = the designers' own firms or their own published words/interviews/lectures
- `[PRESS]` = journalism or trade press
- `[TRADE]` = engineering trade press / engineering firm's own project record

## THE NARRATIVE TURN (read this first)

The single strongest story here: **the design the jury praised is not the design that got built.**
The jury's own January 2004 statement describes visitors *descending below street level* into the
footprints, and mourners reaching *bedrock*. Both were cut in the 2006 cost redesign. The memorial
you stand on is the compromise — Arad's response to losing the thing he thought was the memorial.
See F13–F18. Arad's own line on it: he was afraid that in losing the below-ground memorial, he was
losing the entire memorial (F16).

Second turn: Arad's entry was a **seven-acre stone plaza with no trees**. The jury loved the voids
and disliked the plaza, and required him to take on a landscape architect. The trees — the thing
most visitors think of as the memorial's warmth — were the condition of his winning (F19–F24).

## 1. THE COMPETITION

**F1.** The Lower Manhattan Development Corporation launched the international memorial design
competition in **April 2003**.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "About the Memorial" — https://www.911memorial.org/visit/memorial/about-memorial (accessed 2026-09-23)

**F2.** The competition drew **5,201 submissions from 63 countries**, judged by a **13-person jury**.
This figure is stated identically by the institution and inside the jury's own statement — it is the
most solidly corroborated number in this file.
[INST] 911memorial.org "About the Memorial" (above); and [INST] *WTC Memorial Jury Statement for Winning Design*, January 13, 2004 — https://www.911memorial.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/2004%20jan.13%20WTC%20Memorial%20Jury%20Statement%20for%20Winning%20Design.pdf

**F3.** The jury described its own charge as being handed a near-impossible brief: finding "a single
memorial design only two years after the attacks."
[INST] Jury Statement, Jan 13 2004 (URL above)

**F4.** The jury states it was asked by families and advisory councils to be "especially mindful" of
three things: recognising the victims and those who tried to save them, **keeping the footprints
unencumbered**, and **providing access to the bedrock at Ground Zero**. This is the institution's own
record of what the families demanded.
[INST] Jury Statement, Jan 13 2004

**F5.** The jury also names a fourth, non-commemorative charge: "reintegrating into the urban fabric a
site that had been violently torn from it."
[INST] Jury Statement, Jan 13 2004

**F6.** **Eight finalists** were announced **November 19, 2003**: *Votives in Suspension*; *Lower
Waters*; *Passages of Light: The Memorial Cloud*; *Suspending Memory*; *Garden of Lights*;
*Reflecting Absence*; *Dual Memory*; *Inversion of Light*.
[PRESS] Domus, "Eight finalists for the WTC memorial," 2003-11-20 — https://www.domusweb.it/en/art/2003/11/20/eight-finalists-for-the-wtc-memorial.html ; also ENR 2003-11-19 — https://www.enr.com/articles/30685-wtc-memorial-to-be-chosen-from-eight-designs

**F7.** Crucial detail for the story: the finalist entry was credited to **Michael Arad alone** —
"Reflecting Absence: A Memorial at the World Trade Center Site, by Michael Arad." Peter Walker's
name is not on the finalist entry. He was added afterwards. (See F19.)
[PRESS] Domus finalist list, 2003-11-20 (URL above)

**F8.** *Reflecting Absence* was selected as the winner in **January 2004**; the jury statement is
dated **January 13, 2004**; the design was revealed publicly at a press conference at Federal Hall.
⚠ Dates in circulation differ (Jan 6 selection / Jan 13 statement / Jan 14 public reveal) — see
UNVERIFIED section.
[INST] 911memorial.org "About the Memorial"; [INST] Jury Statement dated 2004-01-13

**F9.** Arad has said he did not feel bound by the competition guidelines the way he believed other
entrants did — he entered without expecting to win.
[PRESS] reporting of Arad's Yale lecture, Yale News, 2012-11-28 — https://news.yale.edu/2012/11/28/architect-911-memorial-tells-story-its-creation

### Quotable jury lines (all ≤20 words, all [INST])
- "By allowing absence to speak for itself, the designers have made the power of these empty footprints the memorial."
- "'Reflecting Absence' has made the voids left by the destruction the primary symbols of our loss."
- "Let this be a place where all of us come together to remember from generation to generation."
- "These trees, like memory itself, demand the care and nurturing of those who visit and tend them."
- "We do not view our selection of a winner as the end of the memorial."
All: [INST] Jury Statement, Jan 13 2004.

## 2. ARAD'S IDEA BEFORE THERE WAS A COMPETITION

**F10.** Arad conceived the voids **in the Hudson River**, in a sketch made shortly after the attacks,
with no competition in existence. In his words: "Shortly after the attack, I sketched this image in
which I imagined a type of memorial in the Hudson River." He describes "coming up to the edge of the
Hudson River and looking across the surface of the water and seeing these two voids which had been
inexplicably formed into the surface of the water."
[DESIGNER] Michael Arad, interviewed by Harel Shapira, *Public Books*, 2013-08-20 — https://www.publicbooks.org/reflecting-absence-an-interviewwith-michael-arad/

**F11.** He calls the exercise private and non-professional: "It was a self-directed, cathartic
exercise. For me, design is process." He has also described the intent as: **"The idea was not to
erase the emptiness, but to give it a presence."** (≤20 words — strong quotable.)
[DESIGNER] Arad in *Public Books*, 2013-08-20

**F12.** In his Yale telling, the Hudson image was of "two voids tearing open a surface of water and
the river failing to fill it up" — conveying "a sense of rupture and continued absence." The idea of
water that *fails to fill* is therefore older than the site, older than the brief. This is the
conceptual seed of the built waterfalls.
[PRESS reporting DESIGNER] Yale News, 2012-11-28 (URL above)

## 3. WHAT THE DESIGN ARGUED AGAINST

**F13.** Daniel Libeskind's *Memory Foundations* master plan had already won the LMDC's site
competition in **2002**, and the memorial guidelines were written afterwards, in relation to it.
Libeskind's plan put the memorial **below street level**, exposing the slurry wall and the bedrock pit.
[DESIGNER] Studio Libeskind project page — https://libeskind.com/work/ground-zero-master-plan/ ; [PRESS] Yale News 2012-11-28

**F14.** Libeskind's own framing of the slurry wall: it "stands as a powerful symbol of endurance and
the human spirit, grounding the project both literally and emotionally in the city's bedrock."
[DESIGNER] Studio Libeskind (URL above)

**F15.** **Arad disregarded the master plan.** He rejected an entirely below-street-level memorial:
"It felt like the site was shielded but also isolated." He substituted an open, street-level public
plaza "punctured by two square voids," reasoning that New Yorkers gather in public where "you can
feel a sense of community and compassion." In his own words elsewhere: **"I wanted it to be firmly
within the city, enmeshed with it and not set apart."**
[PRESS reporting DESIGNER] Yale News, 2012-11-28; [DESIGNER] Arad in *Public Books*, 2013-08-20

**F16.** But the jury's winning design still had a below-grade component — and it was **cut in 2006**.
Arad's original scheme had subterranean galleries inside the footprints where walls of water would
descend over the engraved names. On its loss Arad said: **"I was afraid that in losing this
below-ground memorial, I was losing the entire memorial."**
[PRESS reporting DESIGNER] Yale News, 2012-11-28

**F17.** Arad compared the lost below-ground chambers to **"Orpheus's descent into the Underworld."**
He also described the wider process as **"death by a thousand paper cuts,"** and said of the loss: "I
was incredibly upset," before shifting from "holding onto an idea to finding a way of articulating
the same set of ideas in other ways."
[PRESS] Martin Filler, "The Contentious Ten-Year Saga of Michael Arad's 9/11 Memorial Design," *Metropolis*, 2013-08-05 — https://metropolismag.com/projects/contentious-ten-year-saga-michael-arad-9-11-memorial-design/

**F18.** The mechanism of the cut: after contractors concluded the memorial could cost close to
**$1 billion**, Mayor Bloomberg and Governor Pataki commissioned builder **Frank Sciame** to run a
month-long review in **2006**. Sciame cut **more than $285 million** by shrinking the museum,
**removing the galleries around the pools where the names were to be listed**, consolidating
entrances — and **raising the victims' names to street level**. Target budget: $500 million.
[PRESS] NBC News, "Scaled-down design unveiled for 9/11 memorial," 2006 — https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna13442285 ; [PRESS] CBS News, "Sept. 11 Memorial Cost Jumps To $1B" — https://www.cbsnews.com/news/sept-11-memorial-cost-jumps-to-1b/

> **The load-bearing irony for the script:** the jury statement praises "our descent to the level
> below the street, down into the outlines left by the lost towers," and describes mourning "at
> bedrock of the north tower's footprint." Neither is available to a visitor today. The names the
> jury imagined you would read underground are on the parapet at your elbow. [INST] Jury Statement.

## 4. WHY PETER WALKER WAS ADDED — VOID vs LIVING PLAZA

**F19.** The jury made a landscape architect a **condition**: Arad's finalist scheme needed
collaboration with "a landscape artist of high caliber." Arad, then 34, contacted Walker at his firm
in West Berkeley. Walker joined in **December 2003**, weeks before the win — he had been about to
retire.
[PRESS] Cathy Cockrell, "Design at Ground Zero," UC Berkeley News, 2004-02-25 — https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/berkeleyan/2004/02/25_wtc.shtml ; [PRESS] SFGate, "Peter Walker of PWP talks of 9/11 memorial work" — https://www.sfgate.com/entertainment/article/Peter-Walker-of-PWP-talks-of-9-11-memorial-work-2333161.php

**F20.** Walker's account of the jury's split verdict: jury members **"loved the idea of the big
voids"** — **"But they really disliked seven acres of stone plaza."** The memorial also had to work
as public open space. Walker reduced the stone by roughly half and sculpted the ground with grass
and trees.
[DESIGNER via PRESS] Peter Walker, quoted in SFGate (URL above)

**F21.** **The argument in the designers' own words** — the best single line in this file for a
design-lens beat. Walker described Arad as "dealing with the part of this problem that is about
death," while he himself took "the part of the problem, symbolically, that is about continuation of
life."
[DESIGNER via PRESS] Peter Walker, UC Berkeley News, 2004-02-25

**F22.** Walker wanted seasonal change to do the work: deciduous trees at varied spacing, so
vegetation would convey continuity and transformation. His stated method: **"I try to make one move
produce two or three different effects."**
[DESIGNER via PRESS] UC Berkeley News, 2004-02-25

**F23.** PWP states its own mandate as being to **"humanize the scheme without diminishing the
abstraction"** that had made it a finalist. The grove's trees align "to form arching corridors" that
echo Minoru Yamasaki's original tower arches — i.e. the trees quote the destroyed buildings.
[DESIGNER] PWP Landscape Architecture, project page — https://www.pwpla.com/national-911-memorial/landscape-design

**F24.** The jury then wrote the trees into its own justification, retroactively: the plaza's design
"has evolved to include beautiful groves of trees, traditional affirmations of life and rebirth …
The result is a memorial that expresses both the incalculable loss of life and its consoling
regeneration."
[INST] Jury Statement, Jan 13 2004

**F25.** PWP holds the palette deliberately singular: **one** granite type for cobblestones, pavers
and benches; **a single tree species** throughout the grove. Restraint is the design position.
[DESIGNER] PWP, design process page — https://www.pwpla.com/national-911-memorial/design-process

**F26.** The species is **swamp white oak**, chosen because it is native to the areas of **all three**
9/11 crash sites. Count is given as approximately 400.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "About the Memorial"

**F27.** The Survivor Tree is a **Callery pear**, found in **October 2001** with "snapped roots and
burned and broken branches," rehabilitated by NYC Parks, and returned in **2010**. The institution
notes "new, smooth limbs extended from the gnarled stumps, creating a visible demarcation between
the tree's past and present" — i.e. the tree is legible as a before/after object. It calls it "a
living reminder of resilience, survival, and rebirth."
[INST] 911memorial.org, "Survivor Tree" — https://www.911memorial.org/visit/memorial/survivor-tree

## 5. THE WATER

**F28.** **The line to build the beat around.** Arad's design statement: **"Although water flows into
the voids, they can never be filled."** (11 words.) The full statement continues: "The sound of the
cascading water makes the pools a place of tranquility and contemplation separate from the bustling
noises of the city."
[DESIGNER, quoted in TRADE] ASCE, *Civil Engineering* magazine, "From the ashes: The memorials," September 2021 — https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2021/09/from-the-ashes-the-memorials

**F29.** Water falls **30 feet** into a square basin, then drops **another 20 feet** into a **smaller,
central void** and disappears. The second void is the design's real argument: the water leaves and
does not come back into view. The pools are each **nearly an acre**, in the footprints of the North
and South Towers.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "About the Memorial"

**F30.** Arad's firm describes the **serrated metal edge of the weir** as channelling water "into
separate streams" that evoke "the haunting loss of thousands of individual lives" — the water is
divided deliberately into individual threads.
[DESIGNER] Handel Architects project page — https://handelarchitects.com/project/national-september-11-memorial

**F31.** The weir was developed by water designer **Dan Euser** (Dan Euser Waterarchitecture),
commissioned **2004**, a longtime PWP collaborator. He built **full-scale mock-ups** — a portion of
the waterfall was prototyped at his own studio in **2005** — hunting "the ideal angle, distribution,
speed and volume of water flow," producing "a tapered, rounded weir that is both water-and-energy
efficient as well as highly visible and beautiful."
[DESIGNER] PWP landscape design and design process pages (URLs above)

**F32.** Scale figures: each pool holds roughly **485,919 gallons**; **52,000 gallons recycled per
minute**; parapets run nearly **1,600 linear feet**. ⚠ Press figures conflict (26,000 gpm is widely
repeated) — see UNVERIFIED.
[TRADE] Esther D'Amico, "9/11 Memorial Is Centerpiece of World Trade Center Redevelopment," *Engineering News-Record*, 2011-08-15 — https://www.enr.com/articles/483-9-11-memorial-is-centerpiece-of-world-trade-center-redevelopment

**F33.** PWP describes the pools as the largest manmade waterfalls in North America, and the plaza's
patterned horizontal surfaces (stone, ground cover, lawn, steel grating) as existing to "assert and
reinforce the flatness" — drawing on the firm's "long tradition of designing perceptually flat
landscapes." The flatness is engineered, not incidental.
[DESIGNER] PWP landscape design page; [INST] 911memorial.org "About the Memorial" (largest manmade waterfalls claim)

## 6. THE HARD PROBLEMS — WHAT HAD TO BE INVISIBLE

**F34.** **The plaza is a roof.** The memorial plaza rests on heavy steel framing that also forms the
roof of the museum and the chiller plant, and carries up to **6 feet** of landscaping soil so the
park can be planted with trees. The museum descends as far as **70 feet** below plaza level.
WSP's own phrase for what it built: **"a vast invisible structure."**
[TRADE] WSP, project record — https://www.wsp.com/en-nz/projects/national-september-11-memorial-and-museum

**F35.** Pool structure: each void is **200 × 200 feet** and **30 feet** deep, retained by **4-foot**
thick concrete shear walls socketed **up to 15 feet into bedrock** to resist lateral pressure. WSP
notes the pools had to be treated as "an integral part of the overall structure," not as a water
feature laid on top of one.
[TRADE] WSP (URL above)

**F36.** The whole plaza is effectively a **185,000 sq ft green roof** over the subterranean museum
and the **PATH commuter rail line**. Totals: **8,151 tons** of steel, **49,900 cubic yards** of
concrete. Roughly 400 swamp white oaks, **each weighing nearly one ton**, had to be landed on that
roof. Construction manager Lend Lease; MEP engineer Jaros Baum & Bolles.
[TRADE] ENR, 2011-08-15 (URL above)

**F37.** **The best "invisible decision" for a design beat:** Arad designed the bronze parapets so the
names could be **touched**. Bronze in open sun would burn a hand in summer and risk frostbite in
winter. So a concealed network of copper tubes circulates heated or chilled water behind the plates,
with tube spacing tuned by computational fluid dynamics modelling. MEP engineer Robert Downward:
**"The spacing between the tubes was critical to maintaining comfortable temperatures."** The
prototype was developed by Service Metal Fabricating of Rockaway, N.J.
[TRADE] ENR, 2011-08-15 (URL above)

**F38.** Reported cost of that touchability: **$3.5 million** budgeted for the heating/cooling pipes,
**$4.3 million** as installed, excluding maintenance. ⚠ Cost figures are press, not institutional —
see UNVERIFIED.
[PRESS] Untapped New York, "The Top 10 Secrets of the 9/11 Memorial" — https://www.untappedcities.com/the-top-10-secrets-of-the-911-memorial-in-nyc/

**F39.** The names are **stencil-cut through the bronze** and **illuminated from within at night**, so
each name glows. Arad chose **Optima Medium** and had it modified into a stencil, with discrete
pinned supports to hold the counters (the enclosed spaces inside letters like *a* and *b*) — the
typeface had to be re-engineered because cutting all the way through bronze would otherwise drop the
middle out of the letters. Handel describes the names as appearing "as shadows by day and illuminated
at night."
[PRESS] PRWeb, "9/11 Memorial Nameplates Utilize Custom Bronze Extrusions," 2012-06 — https://www.prweb.com/releases/2012/6/prweb9598161.htm ; [DESIGNER] Handel Architects project page

**F40.** Museum pavilion structural trick: engineers distributed the pavilion's vertical loads across
just **12 points**, split between the PATH station and the museum below — because there was almost
nowhere on the site that could take a column.
[PRESS] Gizmodo, on the WTC transit hub build — https://gizmodo.com/how-new-york-city-built-a-massive-3-8-billion-undergro-5837791 ; corroborated in outline by [TRADE] WSP

## 7. FOUGHT OVER, WON AND LOST — THE NAMES

**F41.** Arad's first proposal was to list the dead in **random order**. Families and the police and
fire unions opposed it for years. Arad himself came off it.
[PRESS] NBC News, "Ad is deployed in battle over 9/11 memorial" — https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna16805098 ; [PRESS] *Metropolis*, 2013-08-05

**F42.** The resolution, in Arad's words: "I decided that we should reach out to family members of the
deceased and ask if they wanted the names of their loved ones to be arranged adjacent to other
victims that the deceased had known during their lives." He also framed it as a question put to
families: "are there names of other victims that you would like to see placed next to the name of
the person you lost?"
[DESIGNER] Arad in *Public Books*, 2013-08-20; and [PRESS] Scientific American, "Commemorative Calculus" — https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/september-11-memorial/

**F43.** In **2006** Bloomberg announced the compromise arrangement: grouping by **where people worked
or where they died**, with flight numbers listed, and the names of responding police and fire
companies. This is the concession — location-grouping was the families' and unions' demand; the
adjacency requests were Arad's counter-offer.
[PRESS] NBC News (URL above); [PRESS] Scientific American

**F44.** Arad's "meaningful adjacencies" concept dated to 2004 but was **shelved until Bloomberg took
control** of the project from the LMDC; the letters to survivors asking for relationships were not
sent until **2009**. The delay is the story: the idea won, five years late.
[PRESS] Martin Filler, *Metropolis*, 2013-08-05

**F45.** Volume: **more than 1,600 requests** came in; **more than 1,200 were honoured**.
[PRESS] Scientific American, "Commemorative Calculus" (URL above)

**F46.** Because hand-solving was impossible, **Jake Barton / Local Projects** and data artist **Jer
Thorp** built an algorithm and software tool. It clusters transitively — if A must sit by B and B by
C, then A, B and C are grouped — then fits clusters to the physical panels. Thorp: "Because these are
typographic elements — some are longer, some are shorter — it turns out there are elasticities in
this which made it possible to solve." **The variable length of human names is what made the layout
solvable.**
[DESIGNER] Jer Thorp, "All The Names: Algorithmic Design and the 9/11 Memorial" — https://blprnt.medium.com/all-the-names-algorithmic-design-and-the-9-11-memorial-9fdcd753057c (fetch blocked; quotes taken from [PRESS] Scientific American and PBS NewsHour, "the mathematical complexity of 2982 names" — https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/the-mathematical-complexity-of-2982-names)

**F47.** The institution's own, notably plainer, description: names are arranged by "where they were
at the time of their death and what personal relationships they shared with other victims," including
"requested adjacencies made by family or next of kin." The institution does not publicise the
algorithm or the fight.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "The Meaning Behind Arrangement of the 9/11 Memorial Names" — https://www.911memorial.org/connect/blog/meaning-behind-arrangement-911-memorial-names

**F48.** **2,983 names** are inscribed — 2,977 killed on 9/11 and 6 killed in the 1993 bombing.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "About the Memorial"

## 8. OTHER CONFLICTS ON THE RECORD

**F49.** The LMDC imposed a three-person team with poor chemistry: Arad (young, untested), Walker
(added for the "softening touch of greenery"), and **J. Max Bond Jr.** of Davis Brody Bond, assigned
to supervise Arad. Filler reports "extreme animosity" within the trio, and that press coverage made
Arad look "petulant and abrasive rather than tenacious and principled."
[PRESS] Martin Filler, *Metropolis*, 2013-08-05

**F50.** Arad regarded the pairing of the museum architects with Davis Brody Bond as a **"conflict of
interest,"** believing the museum was being prioritised over the memorial.
[PRESS] Filler, *Metropolis*, 2013-08-05

**F51.** Public dissent was not unanimous in either direction. A 9/11 widow, Monica Iken Murphy, an
early critic, later said: "My Michael is home finally… This is his final resting place." Another
survivor complained to WINS radio that the memorial was "cool" — meaning cold — and wanted "flowers
or pictures."
[PRESS] Filler, *Metropolis*, 2013-08-05

**F52.** The jury pre-empted future meddling: it recommended the Art Commission of the City of New
York advise the Memorial Foundation "on how to protect the integrity of the design," and that
provisions be made for the annual **Tribute in Light**. The jury was defending the scheme from its
own clients in advance.
[INST] Jury Statement, Jan 13 2004

**F53.** The design was later **reopened by its own authors**: Arad and Walker designed the
**Memorial Glade**, dedicated **May 30, 2019** (the 17th anniversary of the end of the recovery
effort), for those sickened or killed by exposure at Ground Zero. Six stone monoliths of **13 to 18
tons**, with recovered **World Trade Center steel** melted down by a Missouri foundry and poured into
fissures cut in the stone. The institution says the "rough edges and large size were created to honor
the challenges faced by those whom the Glade memorializes." Those monoliths sit on the roof slab over
the museum, requiring high-density foam, concrete and rebar beneath to spread the load.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "9/11 Memorial Glade" — https://www.911memorial.org/visit/memorial/memorial-glade

**F54.** The memorial opened **September 11, 2011**, the tenth anniversary.
[INST] 911memorial.org, "About the Memorial"

## INSTITUTION'S OWN CLAIMS vs PRESS — the split

**The institution asserts (safe to attribute to the 9/11 Memorial & Museum):**
5,201 entries / 63 countries / 13 jurors; April 2003 launch; January 2004 selection; Sept 11 2011
opening; pool size and the 30ft + 20ft water drop; ~400 swamp white oaks and the three-crash-sites
rationale; the Survivor Tree narrative; 2,983 names; "meaningful adjacencies" as a concept; the
Memorial Glade. The full **jury statement** is hosted by the institution and is therefore the single
best primary document in this file — it is both contemporaneous (Jan 13 2004) and institutional.

**The institution does NOT tell these — they are press or designer accounts only:**
- That the winning design was substantially cut in 2006 (the Sciame review, the $1bn scare, the
  removal of the below-grade galleries, the raising of names to street level). [PRESS]
- That Arad deliberately disregarded Libeskind's master plan. [DESIGNER/PRESS]
- That the jury disliked the stone plaza and required a landscape architect. [DESIGNER/PRESS]
- The animosity within the imposed design team, and Arad's "conflict of interest" complaint. [PRESS]
- The names fight (random order, union opposition, Bloomberg's 2006 compromise, the five-year shelving). [PRESS]
- The parapet thermal system's cost. [PRESS]
- The Hudson River origin sketch. [DESIGNER — Arad's own telling, in interview/lecture, not on 911memorial.org]

**Practical consequence for the script:** the narrative turn (F13–F18) rests entirely on press and
the designer's own recollection, set against the institution's own 2004 jury statement. That
juxtaposition is legitimate and strong — the jury document itself proves what the built memorial no
longer does — but do not attribute the *cut* to the institution.

## UNVERIFIED / CONFLICTING — do not use without resolving

1. **Exact win date.** Jan 6, 2004 (selection, per some press), Jan 13, 2004 (jury statement date),
   Jan 14, 2004 (public reveal at Federal Hall). The institution says only "January 2004." **Use
   "January 2004."**
2. **Tree count: 400 vs 416.** 911memorial.org, Handel Architects and ENR all say ~400; PWP's own page
   says "a dense forest of 416 oak trees." Both are designer/institution sources and they disagree.
