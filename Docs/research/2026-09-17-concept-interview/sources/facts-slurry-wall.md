# Fact sheet — the slurry wall under the 9/11 Memorial plaza

Sources for the `memorial_dig` beat in `../narration.md`. **Everything below was fetched and
read directly in this session (2026-09-23)** — the two primary documents were downloaded and
their text extracted here, not summarised from anywhere. Quotations are verbatim.

A judge review blocked the first version of that beat because these facts appeared in no
fact sheet in the repo. This file is the fix: the claims were sound, the paper trail was not.

## Primary documents

1. **George J. Tamaro**, "World Trade Center 'Bathtub': From Genesis to Armageddon",
   *The Bridge*, National Academy of Engineering, Vol. 32 No. 1, Spring 2002, pp. 11–17.
   Full-issue PDF: `https://www.nae.edu/File.aspx?id=7345&v=fa48f980` (the `.aspx` article page
   is a JavaScript stub and returns no text; the issue PDF does). Tamaro was the Port
   Authority engineer on the original wall and led the post-9/11 assessment.
2. **Arturo Ressi**, audio-transcript oral history, 9/11 Memorial & Museum —
   `https://www.911memorial.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/Arturo Ressi Transcript.pdf`.
   Ressi worked on the original slurry-wall construction for Icanda, the contractor.

## S1 — 1,500 tieback anchors held the wall while they dug
> "To provide lateral support of the wall as the excavation proceeded downward, 1,500
> high-strength tendon tieback anchors were installed. Four to six tiers of tieback anchors
> were installed through sleeves ("trumpets") in the slurry wall, drilled through the soil
> using steel pipe casing, and then drilled 30 to 35 feet into bedrock."
— Tamaro, p. 12. Anchor capacities 100–300 tons; ~55 extra anchors replaced ones obstructed,
damaged or failing test.

## S2 — the anchors were let go once the floors could do the job
> "Once the permanent basement floors were capable of supporting the walls, the tieback
> anchors were detensioned and the sleeves sealed."
— Tamaro, p. 13. **Tamaro's word is "detensioned."** Ressi, who was there, says they were
cut, and explains why (S3). Narration says "cut", on Ressi.

## S3 — why they were cut, and what that meant on 9/11
> "The design was that when the floor system of the basement of the World Trade Center went
> against the wall, those anchors, those tieback, would be cut, because if they had failed
> differentially, one would have failed and then one near it not, they would have put
> differential stresses on the wall. So in order not to have that, they were all cut. So when
> the building collapsed and destroyed the floor system, the wall in many area was
> unsupported, because the floor system was not there and the tieback had been cut. **So it
> should have collapsed.**"
— Ressi, oral history [01:34].

## S4 — what failure would have meant
> "The slurry wall would have failed, the Hudson River would have come into the site and
> through the PATH tubes, would have gone to New Jersey, come back on 35th and flooded all
> the subway system of New York. And the loss of life would have been incredible."
— Ressi [02:00]. *Not voiced in the beat; the PATH plugs (S5) carry the same point as fact.*

## S5 — the concrete plugs in the PATH tubes
> "Within days, a 16-foot long low-strength concrete plug was placed in each tube as a seal
> in the event that the bathtub walls were breached and the tunnels fully flooded. The plugs
> were designed to withstand an 80-foot head of water pressure."
— Tamaro, p. 15. As much as 3,000 gallons a minute was being pumped from the north tunnel
for a 12-hour period each day.

## S6 — the spoil became Battery Park City
> "More than a million cubic yards of excavation spoil was carted to a disposal area across
> West Street and eventually incorporated into the landfill for Battery Park City."
— Tamaro, p. 13. Narration says "more than a million", matching this; a separate ASCE figure
of 1.2 million cubic yards and ~23.5 acres agrees in magnitude.

## S7 — the wall after the collapse
> "Along the south wall at Liberty Street, the majority of the wall was unsupported for most
> of its 60-foot height."
— Tamaro, p. 16. Tension cracks opened in Liberty Street south of the wall.

## Not voiced, and why
- **How far the wall moved.** Tamaro says the top moved more than 10 inches; other credible
  sources say over 2 feet and over 4 feet. No figure is spoken.
- **The wall's exact dimensions.** "Three feet thick and seventy feet deep" follows ASCE's
  *Civil Engineering* (3 ft thick, 22 ft wide panels, 70 ft deep); Tamaro describes the wall
  as socketed into rock at depths of as much as 80 feet, which is the socket, not the height.
- **Who the Port Authority's chief engineer was.** Tamaro names no one; the only named
  engineer in a citable source is Martin Kapp, chief geotechnical engineer.
